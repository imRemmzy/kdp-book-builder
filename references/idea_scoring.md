# Idea generation and scoring (Phase 1)

## Brainstorm

Produce 15–25 candidates. If the user gave an area, stay inside it but spread across genres (a how-to, a reference, a puzzle book, a journal all about gardening). If they gave nothing, spread across evergreen categories that suit research-based production: practical how-to, beginner guides, reference and checklists, niche hobbies, activity and puzzle books, journals with meaningful guided content, local history, plain-language explainers.

Screen out immediately (don't score): anything built on a trademark or franchise, "summary of / workbook for / companion to" someone else's book, books about a living celebrity, topics that need credentials the user doesn't have to be safe (e.g. medication guides), and trend topics that will be stale before publication.

## Market research checklist (per candidate)

Use web search. Amazon pages often can't be fetched directly; when they can't, use search-result snippets, publisher pages, Goodreads, and review aggregators, and **say the figures are rough**.

- **Reader:** who they are, what problem or desire brings them to the book, what they'd type into Amazon search.
- **Competition:** 5–10 closest books: title, author type (trad publisher / indie), approximate review count, publication year, format and price. Note if the top books are old (opportunity) or recent and strong (harder).
- **Gaps:** recurring complaints in 2–3 star reviews (outdated, too basic, no pictures, errors, poorly formatted Kindle version). These are the angle.
- **Demand shape:** evergreen, seasonal (note the season and lead time), or trend-driven.
- **Feasibility:** can a genuinely good book be written from verifiable public sources, without personal expertise the user doesn't have? Does it need original photos, lived experience, or professional judgement?

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each; higher is always better)

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | Few people search for this; competitors have almost no reviews | Steady niche interest; competitors have dozens to low hundreds of reviews | Clearly large, steady interest; many competitors with hundreds+ of reviews |
| **Competition** (5 = low) | Dominated by strong, recent books from major publishers | A few decent books, some dated or weak | Few competitors, or most are weak/outdated/poorly reviewed |
| **Differentiation** | Nothing to add beyond what exists | A clear improvement on one axis (newer, clearer, better visuals) | A distinct angle readers are asking for in reviews |
| **Research feasibility** | Needs expertise, primary data or experiences we can't get | Mostly public sources, a few gaps | Fully supportable from authoritative public sources |
| **Evergreen value** | Stale within a year | Needs updating every 2–3 years | Useful for 5+ years |
| **Risk** (5 = low) | Health/legal/finance advice, trademark proximity, real people | Some safety or accuracy sensitivity | Low stakes if a detail is imperfect; no IP issues |

**Total = sum of the six (max 30).** Show the six numbers and the sum for every candidate so the user can see the reasoning, e.g. `Demand 4 + Competition 3 + Differentiation 4 + Feasibility 5 + Evergreen 4 + Risk 5 = 25`.

Don't weight silently. If you think one criterion should dominate for this user (e.g. Risk for a first book), say so and show both totals.

## Presenting the top 5

A table: rank, working title, score, format, suggested length, price range. Then one paragraph per idea:

- **Working title** and **subtitle** (honest, no brand names)
- **Target reader** in one sentence
- **Angle** that beats the existing books, tied to the gaps found
- **Suggested length** (pages and words), **format** (eBook, paperback, both), **price range** (eBook and paperback, from competitor prices)
- **What's uncertain:** which numbers are rough, and what the user should check themselves on Amazon (e.g. the Best Sellers Rank of the top 5 competitors: under roughly 100,000 in the Kindle store suggests steady sales; treat any such threshold as a rule of thumb, not a fact)

End with the checkpoint question: which idea to pursue, or more ideas.
