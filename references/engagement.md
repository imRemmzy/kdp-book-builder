# Writing for engagement

Use in Phase 4 (drafting) and Phase 5 (quality pass). The goal is a book a reader finishes and recommends, written from the research file only.

## Chapter anatomy

1. **Hook** (first 1–3 sentences): one of
   - a surprising fact from the research file (with its footnote),
   - a relatable problem stated in the reader's words,
   - a short scenario, labelled if hypothetical ("Imagine you…"),
   - the question this chapter answers.
2. **Payoff promise**: say early what the reader will be able to do or understand ("By the end of this chapter you'll be able to…"). One sentence; don't oversell.
3. **Body**: concrete examples, numbered steps, specific numbers, worked cases. One idea per paragraph.
4. **Scannable elements** where the genre allows: checklists, tip boxes (a blockquote starting with a bold label such as `> **Tip:**`), short summary tables.
5. **Recap and pull**: two to four lines recapping what was covered, then one line that makes the next chapter feel necessary.

## Style

- Plain language at the reading level set in Phase 2. Define jargon the first time it appears, in the same sentence if possible.
- Vary rhythm: mix short and long sentences. Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences) in how-to and explainer books.
- Specific beats vague: "water until it drains from the bottom, usually every 7–10 days in summer [^2]" beats "water regularly".
- Active voice and second person ("you") for instructions.
- One clear way of saying each point. Don't restate the same point three ways.
- Honest anecdotes only: hypotheticals are labelled; invented stories are never presented as real events or real people.
- Keep the reader's time sacred: if a paragraph doesn't teach, show, or move the story, cut it.

## Banned AI tells

Search the draft for these in Phase 5 and rewrite any hit. They make readers (and reviewers) suspect low-effort AI content.

**Words and phrases:** delve, tapestry, testament to, in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-evolving landscape, navigate the complexities, embark on a journey, unlock the secrets, unleash, elevate, game-changer, a treasure trove, it's important to note that, it's worth noting, in conclusion (as a paragraph opener), ultimately (as a filler), furthermore/moreover (chains of them), plays a crucial/pivotal role, a myriad of, harness the power, seamless(ly), robust (outside technical use), vibrant, bustling, nestled, whether you're a beginner or a seasoned pro, look no further, dive in / deep dive, the world of X, at the end of the day, a rich history, stands as, serves as a reminder.

**Patterns:**
- "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions.
- Rhetorical question → answer openers in every section.
- Triplets everywhere ("fast, simple, and effective").
- Every paragraph the same length and shape.
- Hollow inspirational wrap-ups ("With these tips, you're ready to conquer the world of…").
- Em-dash overuse; prefer commas, colons, full stops and parentheses.
- Summaries that restate the chapter instead of consolidating it.

## Front and back matter checklist

- Title page (generated by the builders from `book.json`)
- Copyright page (template in `compliance.md` §7), with disclaimer if needed and research date
- Table of contents (generated)
- Introduction: who it's for, what they'll get, how to use the book
- Chapters
- Conclusion: what the reader can now do, and a next step. Not a recap of every chapter.
- Sources / notes (footnotes become per-chapter notes in the EPUB and numbered endnotes in print)
- About the author: only true statements; generic for a pen name
- Optional honest review request: one or two sentences, e.g. "If this book helped, a short, honest review helps other readers find it." No incentives, no asking only for five stars.
